## What's New

- **The start screen is a game library** — Your games are listed on the left with how many of your maps each has; the one you pick shows on the right with its picture, four ways to start a map (Make me a map among them, picked out so it is easy to find), your maps for it and the map packs found for it. WadSmith opens on the game you used last.

- **3D models as props** — "Add a 3D model…" on the Objects shelf, or a model file dropped on the plan or the 3D view, brings in OBJ, glTF and GLB, MD3 and IQM models, and Quake's MDL and MD2 models in Quake's own colours. Each becomes an object of its own, drawn as the model in the 3D view and in GZDoom, with eight Doom-style pictures made from it for the plan and the shelf. The Inspector's "Model faces" turns one made facing another way. With Quake installed, its own models are on the Objects shelf when you pick Quake in the game chooser.
- **Voxels and block sets** — MagicaVoxel (.vox) and KVX voxel models come in as props the same way, and a set of block textures (a top, sides and bottom) becomes a cube.
- **Your own pictures, animated GIFs and materials on walls, doors and floors** — "Add a picture or material…" on the Textures shelf brings in PNG, JPEG, WebP and animated GIFs; each is one tile under "Your pictures", and GIFs play in the 3D view. Dropped on a wall a picture takes just the space its shape needs, once, like a poster; on a door or a floor it fills it. "Fit the picture to this wall" and "Fit the floor picture to this room" size a picture to cover it once. PBR material sets (colour with normal, roughness, metal and occlusion maps) come in as textures that GZDoom lights as materials.
- **Skies from a 360° picture** — Map settings → "Make a sky from a 360° picture…" turns a panorama or an HDRI photograph into the map's sky.
- **Wolfenstein 3D levels** — Map settings → "Bring in a Wolfenstein 3D level…" makes a map of a level from your own copy of the game.

- **Tools for mappers who know other editors** — Ctrl+A selects everything (or every corner, wall, room or object when the Select tool picks one kind), Ctrl+I selects the rest instead, and Ctrl+L brings back the selection you just lost. "Select alike" picks the rooms with the same floor or ceiling picture, height or brightness, the rooms joined to one at the same floor height, the walls with the same picture, the walls a wall runs on into, or every object of a kind; it is in the Inspector under "Like this one" and in Ctrl+K. Ctrl+Shift+C and Ctrl+Shift+V copy one room's, wall's or object's look and settings onto others, keeping their tags and ids. [ and ] set the grid (1 to 1024 units), Shift+Home shows the selection, and ' on the plan puts the 3D view there. In the 3D view, Ctrl+C on a wall, floor or ceiling picks its picture up and Ctrl+V puts it on another.
- **Shape and move exactly** — "Snap to the grid", "Line the corners up evenly", "Space the corners round a circle" (a room becomes round), "Move east by" and "Move north by" in the Inspector and Ctrl+K; "Split it in half" for a wall and "Swap the floor and ceiling pictures" for a room.
- **More settings by name** — A room's effect is chosen from a list: flickering, glowing or blinking light, floors that hurt, heal or kill, ice, a door that shuts by itself, lightning. Objects can be left out of single player, co-op or deathmatch, made friendly or asleep until woken, and given an id; walls can block sound or players, or show on the in-game map from the start.
- **See the light** — The plan's new Brightness view paints every room in a grey as bright as its light, so dark corners stand out. Ctrl+K "Next map" and "Previous map" go through the maps of a project.
- **More checks** — Problems calls out objects that appear on no difficulty, with a fix that puts them on every one. For a map that plays in vanilla Doom it also calls out fewer than four deathmatch starts, see-through middles the original engine smears (the Medusa effect, with a fix), and walls showing more picture than they have or holes in it (tutti-frutti).


- **Make me a map** — Each game's card has "Make me a map…": WadSmith builds a whole map for that game while you choose, and opens it as an ordinary map you can change like any other. Choose which of your games it takes textures, monsters, weapons and music from (Duke Nukem 3D and Quake lend their textures), the size, the style (tech-base rooms, caves with their corners cut, castle halls with pillars, or a mix), how much is open to the sky, the challenge in your game's own difficulty names, how many locked doors and bosses (none to three), and secrets behind doors that look like wall, stairs, lifts, teleporters, dark places, ambushes, monster closets that open behind you, water and lava pools and decorations. Keys stand on a dais in rooms of their own before their doors, and every map is checked to be finishable, with monsters for every difficulty and enough ammunition for them. Two words make the map (the same words and choices make the same map anywhere), and a map can be one of an episode of 3, 5 or 8 that rises in challenge. The map is saved in a folder you choose in Preferences and listed on its game's card; Ctrl+K "Make another like it" makes the next one. `wadsmith-cli generate` makes them without the window.

- **Things from your other games** — The Objects and Textures shelves have a game chooser: this map's own game, or any other game you have. Monsters, weapons, decorations and pickups from Heretic, Hexen and Strife drag into a Doom map like its own: their pictures and sounds, and those of what they fire, drop or spawn, are copied into the project the first time, and GZDoom plays them as they are in their own game. A weapon goes into the slot it has in its own game; its ammunition shows on GZDoom's other HUDs, not on Doom's status bar. Duke Nukem 3D's art is there too when WadSmith finds its DUKE3D.GRP: every tile, copied into the project in its own colours when you use it, and its enemies — the Assault Trooper, the Pig Cop, the Enforcer, the Octabrain, the Assault Commander and the Battlelord — which look like Duke's and behave like a Doom monster (a Pig Cop fights like a shotgun guy), since Duke's own behaviour lives in scripts GZDoom cannot run. Quake's wall textures are there too when WadSmith finds its PAK files. Objects from another game make the map GZDoom-only.

- **Maps for vanilla Doom and the classic ports (first part)** — Each game card has "Plays in": GZDoom (everything WadSmith can make), Vanilla Doom (the 1993 engine's format, as Chocolate Doom and the DOS game play it) or Limit-removing (that format without the limits, as Crispy Doom plays it); Map settings changes it later. With a classic target the project keeps its map in the classic format, written back from the editor through GZDoom's own table of what each classic line type means, and Problems lists anything that has no classic type. WadSmith builds the map's nodes itself on every save for a classic target, as the classic ports need; their limits as checks and playing in the port itself come next.

- **Your WADs are all you need** — WadSmith now looks where GZDoom's own settings look, inside ZIP files (taking a base game out when one is in there), and around any folder you point it at: the folder, the one above it and the ones beside it. A large collection is read once and remembered.
- **Pick a game file** — Point WadSmith straight at DOOM2.WAD, HERETIC.WAD or a ZIP holding one, on the start screen or in Preferences.
- **Cover pictures** — Each game's tile shows the game's cover, fetched once from its Wikipedia article and kept; the game's own title screen shows until it arrives, and when there is none. Preferences → "Show each game's cover picture" turns it off, and then nothing is fetched.
- **Maps found near your games** — Map packs in your folders (WADs with maps, loose or inside ZIPs) are matched to the game they are for, by their map names, their maps' namespace and the textures they use, and listed on that game's tile with a search box; pick a map to edit it. The pack is loaded with your project in the editor and when you play, so its own textures and things are there. A big collection is read once and remembered, so later starts are quick.
- **Your maps, on the game they are for** — Each game's tile has "Your maps": open one, rename it, copy it or delete it (into a Deleted folder, never gone). Maps whose game is not found right now are listed under the tiles.
- **The tools say how** — Pick Room and it says both ways to draw: drag a rectangle, or click corner by corner and double-click the last corner to finish; the Door and Window tools say what to click. While the Guide is open, the card shows the same line.
- **Which base game would you like your map to use?** — The start screen asks the question a first map starts with, over your games.

## What's Fixed

- **Heretic's and Hexen's keys** — The way from start to exit, Problems, "Explain this map" and the Inspector now understand Heretic's and Hexen's own keys (a Hexen key carried over from another map of its hub is no fault), where they used to report a key behind its own door.
- **Bringing in another game's monster is quick** — A monster with all its pictures took a long time to copy on a spinning disk; it is now about a quarter of a second. A monster sharing pictures with one brought in before (Heretic's golem and its ghost) comes too, and Doom II's monsters come into a Doom map.
- **A project of several maps** — The editor's top bar lists a project's maps when it has more than one; pick one to edit it.

- **A corner clicked just short of a room's corner lands on it** — Zoomed in, a click a few units from a room's corner, near its wall, used to leave a new corner beside it, slightly off the grid; the rooms then never quite shared their wall, and a thin sliver of room could end up between them. The corner now goes onto the room's own corner. Problems also calls out a sliver of room left in an older map, with "Join it to the room beside it".
- **Changing a door's look leaves the rooms alone** — On a door built without a doorway, choosing a shorter door picture used to bring the ceilings of the rooms on either side down to the picture's height.

- **A door between two rooms is a door in a wall** — On an opening wider than a door, the Door tool now makes a standard door in the middle and closes the rest with wall either side; a dragged stretch is the door, with wall beside it. "Wall it off" on an opening closes it entirely. A first map used to end with a door standing alone in a gap.
- **A texture on the whole wall, or on just a section** — A texture dropped on a wall covers the whole straight wall, through the pieces a door or a switch cut it into (the switch keeps its picture); hold Shift as you let go and it covers a section as wide as the picture, where you dropped it. The hint and the outline say which, and the Guide teaches it.
- **Lock a door with a key, the way you'd expect** — Drag a key from the Objects shelf onto a door to lock it with that key, or right-click the door and pick "Lock with the red key" (and "Unlock"); the Inspector's "Locked with" is still there. A door locked with a key not yet on the map says where the key must go.
- **Choose how a door looks** — A door's Inspector has "Looks like": a strip of the game's own door pictures; right-clicking the door on the plan offers the same. The doorway takes the picture's height, so the wall shows above a short door and a tall one fills the doorway.
- **The exit switch sits in the wall, not on it** — The exit switch picture is as wide and as tall as it really is (32 × 72 in Doom): it is set into an alcove with the wall above it when the room is taller, so it no longer repeats across and above itself. The same goes for a door's or a lift's switch.
- **What the game cannot show is not offered** — The Objects shelf only offers what the game's own data has pictures for (Doom II's super shotgun is not in Doom's), and Problems calls out one already on the map, with "Take it out". ZDoom's scripted marines are named by what they carry ("Marine with a rocket launcher"), not like weapons.
- **Wall above the door** — In a room taller than the door's picture, the picture used to show again above the door (it hangs from the door's top edge and repeats upward). The Door tool now builds a doorway as tall as the door: a short frame either side of it with the wall above, as Doom's own doors are built. Doors made before this stay as they are; make one again to get the frame.
- **The exit switch is a switch, not a wall of them** — "Make it the exit switch" now takes a piece of the wall as wide as the switch picture, where you clicked the wall (its middle when it was selected another way), and the rest stays wall; the Guide's exit step says so. The whole wall used to be tiled with exit switches.
- **Where two rooms meet is an opening, and it says so** — Rooms drawn against each other open into each other; the plan now draws that line dashed, the tutorial explains it as it happens, "What is this?" and the Inspector name it an opening and offer "Make it a door" and "Make it a window" right there, and drawing a shape along walls already there is answered with how rooms and doors work.
- **Back in the Guide** — A Back button returns to the step before, to read it again.
- **A room drawn against a slanted wall shares it** — A corner that landed a hair off an existing wall used to leave two walls lying along each other, so no door could go between the rooms. Now the corner goes onto the wall and the rooms share that stretch.
- **The guided first map checks the rooms touch** — Its room step waits for a room that shares a wall with the first and says what to do when the new room does not; what WadSmith refuses shows on the Guide's card, not only in the status bar.
- **The Guide's buttons stay readable** — With a long game name the button row ran off the card; it wraps now.
- **Door and Window tools say "click on a wall"** — A click that lands on nothing no longer does nothing in silence, and the tools reach twice as far for a wall.

- **"No game" no longer means "get Freedoom"** — When a folder you added holds no base game, the start screen says what it did hold (WADs, archives, map files by name) and what a base game is; Freedoom is offered last, not first.
- **Flatpak GZDoom** — Its data file is found, so the Objects shelf has its monsters and items; the games folder Debian uses is looked in as well.
