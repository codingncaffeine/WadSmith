## What's New

- **Things from your other games** — The Objects and Textures shelves have a game chooser: this map's own game, or any other game you have. Monsters, weapons, decorations and pickups from Heretic, Hexen and Strife drag into a Doom map like its own: their pictures and sounds, and those of what they fire, drop or spawn, are copied into the project the first time, and GZDoom plays them as they are in their own game. A weapon goes into the slot it has in its own game; its ammunition shows on GZDoom's other HUDs, not on Doom's status bar. Duke Nukem 3D's art is there too when WadSmith finds its DUKE3D.GRP: every tile, copied into the project in its own colours when you use it. Objects from another game make the map GZDoom-only.

- **Maps for vanilla Doom and the classic ports (first part)** — Each game card has "Plays in": GZDoom (everything WadSmith can make), Vanilla Doom (the 1993 engine's format, as Chocolate Doom and the DOS game play it) or Limit-removing (that format without the limits, as Crispy Doom plays it); Map settings changes it later. With a classic target the project keeps its map in the classic format, written back from the editor through GZDoom's own table of what each classic line type means, and Problems lists anything that has no classic type. WadSmith builds the map's nodes itself on every save for a classic target, as the classic ports need; their limits as checks and playing in the port itself come next.

- **Your WADs are all you need** — WadSmith now looks where GZDoom's own settings look, inside ZIP files (taking a base game out when one is in there), and around any folder you point it at: the folder, the one above it and the ones beside it. A large collection is read once and remembered.
- **Pick a game file** — Point WadSmith straight at DOOM2.WAD, HERETIC.WAD or a ZIP holding one, on the start screen or in Preferences.
- **Cover pictures** — Each game's tile shows the game's cover, fetched once from its Wikipedia article and kept; the game's own title screen shows until it arrives, and when there is none. Preferences → "Show each game's cover picture" turns it off, and then nothing is fetched.
- **Maps found near your games** — Map packs in your folders (WADs with maps, loose or inside ZIPs) are matched to the game they are for, by their map names, their maps' namespace and the textures they use, and listed on that game's tile with a search box; pick a map to edit it. The pack is loaded with your project in the editor and when you play, so its own textures and things are there. A big collection is read once and remembered, so later starts are quick.
- **Your maps, on the game they are for** — Each game's tile has "Your maps": open one, rename it, copy it or delete it (into a Deleted folder, never gone). Maps whose game is not found right now are listed under the tiles.
- **The tools say how** — Pick Room and it says both ways to draw: drag a rectangle, or click corner by corner and double-click the last corner to finish; the Door and Window tools say what to click. While the Guide is open, the card shows the same line.
- **Which base game would you like your map to use?** — The start screen asks the question a first map starts with, over your games.

## What's Fixed

- **A door between two rooms is a door in a wall** — On an opening wider than a door, the Door tool now makes a standard door in the middle and closes the rest with wall either side; a dragged stretch is the door, with wall beside it. "Wall it off" on an opening closes it entirely. A first map used to end with a door standing alone in a gap.
- **A texture on the whole wall, or on just a section** — A texture dropped on a wall covers the whole straight wall, through the pieces a door or a switch cut it into (the switch keeps its picture); hold Shift as you let go and it covers a section as wide as the picture, where you dropped it. The hint and the outline say which, and the Guide teaches it.
- **Lock a door with a key, the way you'd expect** — Drag a key from the Objects shelf onto a door to lock it with that key, or right-click the door and pick "Lock with the red key" (and "Unlock"); the Inspector's "Locked with" is still there. A door locked with a key not yet on the map says where the key must go.
- **Choose how a door looks** — A door's Inspector has "Looks like": a strip of the game's own door pictures; right-clicking the door on the plan offers the same. The doorway takes the picture's height, so the wall shows above a short door and a tall one fills the doorway.
- **The exit switch sits in the wall, not on it** — The exit switch picture is as wide and as tall as it really is (32 × 72 in Doom): it is set into an alcove with the wall above it when the room is taller, so it no longer repeats across and above itself. The same goes for a door's or a lift's switch.
- **What the game cannot show is not offered** — The Objects shelf only offers what the game's own data has pictures for (Doom II's super shotgun is not in Doom's), and Problems calls out one already on the map, with "Take it out". ZDoom's scripted marines are named by what they carry ("Marine with a rocket launcher"), not like weapons.
- **Wall above the door** — In a room taller than the door's picture, the picture used to show again above the door (it hangs from the door's top edge and repeats upward). The Door tool now builds a doorway as tall as the door: a short frame either side of it with the wall above, as Doom's own doors are built. Doors made before this stay as they are; make one again to get the frame.
- **The exit switch is a switch, not a wall of them** — "Make it the exit switch" now takes a piece of the wall as wide as the switch picture, where you clicked the wall (its middle when it was selected another way), and the rest stays wall; the Guide's exit step says so. The whole wall used to be tiled with exit switches.
- **Where two rooms meet is an opening, and it says so** — Rooms drawn against each other open into each other; the plan now draws that line dashed, the tutorial explains it as it happens, "What is this?" and the Inspector name it an opening and offer "Make it a door" and "Make it a window" right there, and drawing a shape along walls already there is answered with how rooms and doors work.
- **Back in the Guide** — A Back button returns to the step before, to read it again.
- **A room drawn against a slanted wall shares it** — A corner that landed a hair off an existing wall used to leave two walls lying along each other, so no door could go between the rooms. Now the corner goes onto the wall and the rooms share that stretch.
- **The guided first map checks the rooms touch** — Its room step waits for a room that shares a wall with the first and says what to do when the new room does not; what WadSmith refuses shows on the Guide's card, not only in the status bar.
- **The Guide's buttons stay readable** — With a long game name the button row ran off the card; it wraps now.
- **Door and Window tools say "click on a wall"** — A click that lands on nothing no longer does nothing in silence, and the tools reach twice as far for a wall.

- **"No game" no longer means "get Freedoom"** — When a folder you added holds no base game, the start screen says what it did hold (WADs, archives, map files by name) and what a base game is; Freedoom is offered last, not first.
- **Flatpak GZDoom** — Its data file is found, so the Objects shelf has its monsters and items; the games folder Debian uses is looked in as well.
